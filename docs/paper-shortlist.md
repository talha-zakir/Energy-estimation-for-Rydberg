# Paper shortlist

Sources checked 2026-11-02. “Rydberg data,” “Rydberg simulator,” and “execution on a Rydberg device” are different claims. The table distinguishes them.

| Priority | Paper / primary source | What was implemented | Hardware evidence | Fit for this project |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 — actual analog variational hardware route | DiBrita, Han and Patel, **ResQ: A Novel Framework to Implement Residual Neural Networks on Analog Rydberg Atom Quantum Computers**, ICCV 2025. [Paper](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/ICCV2025/papers/DiBrita_ResQ_A_Novel_Framework_to_Implement_Residual_Neural_Networks_on_ICCV_2025_paper.pdf), [author code](https://github.com/positivetechnologylab/ResQ) | Trainable analog pulses encode input features and model parameters; MNIST, FashionMNIST and healthcare binary classification. | Section 5: training is simulated, hardware inference uses four atoms on Aquila with 1,000 shots per task. Section 6.4: eight selected inputs per classification task. This is limited hardware inference, not hardware training or a full hardware accuracy benchmark. | Best shortlist candidate if actual analog-device execution is the primary requirement. It uses experimentally available local detuning in the paper; current access needs checking. Adding a regression head is a modification. |
| 2 — recommended first VQC simulation baseline | Lu et al., **Digital-analog quantum learning on Rydberg atom arrays**. [arXiv:2401.02940v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2401.02940v2), [journal DOI](https://doi.org/10.1088/2058-9565/ad9177), [author code](https://github.com/jz-lu/daqml) | Local trainable rotations alternate with fixed global Rydberg evolution; binary MNIST and quantum phase-boundary learning. | Numerical study with hardware considerations and noise models; not a demonstrated hardware-trained MNIST VQC. | Most direct, simple reference for our first circuit and physical constants. **Implemented here as an explicitly modified baseline.** Requires digital–analog controls for hardware transfer. |
| 3 — experimental fixed-reservoir comparison | Kornjača et al., **Large-scale quantum reservoir learning with an analog quantum computer**. [arXiv:2407.02553](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.02553), [full methods](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.02553v1), [official tutorials](https://github.com/QuEraComputing/QRC-tutorials) | Input-dependent Rydberg dynamics produce measured features for a classical readout; includes MNIST. | Actual neutral-atom experiments. | Strong hardware reference for measured features and readout. **Not a VQC**: no iterative training of quantum circuit parameters. The paper's MNIST readout is an SVM, so our regression head would not reproduce that experiment. |
| 4 — different-task hardware comparison | Antoncich et al., **Quantum Reservoir Computing with Neutral Atoms on a Small, Complex, Medical Dataset**. [arXiv:2602.14641](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.14641) | Rydberg features on a small biomarker dataset, evaluated with classical predictors. | Both noiseless emulation and Aquila execution. | Useful precedent for moving beyond MNIST, data-split variation and hardware regularization. Also **reservoir computing, not variational quantum training**. |
| 5 — alternative trainable architecture | Agarwal et al., **Extending Quantum Perceptrons: Rydberg Devices, Multi-Class Classification, and Error Tolerance**. [arXiv:2411.09093](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.09093), [full text](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.09093v1) | Trainable perceptron dynamics for quantum phase and entanglement classification, with multi-output extensions. | Simulation/proposed experimental realization; do not treat the discussion of an experiment as evidence of a device run. | Relevant alternative, but a less direct starting point for a classical-image pipeline and regression readout. |

## Recommendation

Use Lu et al. to establish a fast, testable VQC simulation reference with its physical constants held fixed. Use ResQ as the leading route to an analog-hardware implementation, and Kornjača et al. as a fixed-reservoir hardware comparator. Do not present these as equivalent implementations: their controls, encoding, readout, and training procedures differ.

The public Lu code was inspected at commit `51ce418660a720c3116bf0dbc3c220622ef9b37a`, specifically `MNIST_classification/gates.jl`, `binary_classifier.jl`, and `prep_full.ipynb`. No upstream source files are vendored here.

## Excluded near-match

[Quantum Phase Classification of Rydberg Atom Systems Using Resource-Efficient Variational Quantum Circuits and Classical Shadows](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.23489) trains a small VQC on features obtained from Rydberg states. That does not establish execution of the classifier itself on Rydberg hardware, so it is not our hardware implementation baseline.
